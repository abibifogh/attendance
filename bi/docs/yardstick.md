# The Yardstick

Every other screen in this app reports what happened. This one compares — which
is the difference between a dashboard and something a decision can be made
against.

---

## The one number it needs from you

**Setup → The cost no system records.** Rent, power, water, licences,
depreciation: one figure, in cedis a month.

None of these is in the tills, the clock, the laundry, the kitchen or the books,
because none of those is where a lease lives. Until it is set, the break-even on
this screen counts wages and nothing else, and is understated by exactly the
rent. The screen says so, in a banner, every time.

A rough figure is worth far more than none. A break-even that is approximately
right beats one that is precisely wrong by the whole of the rent.

---

## What it says, and why each is there

### What a day has to take to pay for itself

Purchases are treated as varying with takings — food bought is food sold — and
wages as fixed, because a rota is set a week ahead and does not shrink because
Tuesday was quiet. Both are measured from the warehouse, not modelled.

Break-even is the fixed cost divided by what survives of each cedi. Margin of
safety is how far takings could fall before the profit is gone, and goes
**negative** rather than clamping to zero, because how far under is the only
thing worth knowing at that point.

Operating leverage is withheld where there is no profit. The arithmetic still
produces a number on a loss-making month, and "−6×" reads as a fact about
volatility when it is a division by a negative.

### Why this period differs from the last

Four bars that sum to the change, exactly:

| Bar | What it means |
|---|---|
| Money through the door | The same margins on more or less money |
| Which lines earned it | The same total, earned by a different mix of lines |
| How well they earned | The same lines and shares, earning differently |
| Support-line costs | Housekeeping, maintenance, admin costing more or less |

The fourth exists because the first three are built on revenue share, and a line
with no revenue has no share and no margin — so a rise in what housekeeping
costs is structurally invisible to them. Before it existed, that rise was being
absorbed silently onto whichever other bar happened to be largest.

If the bars ever fail to add up, a fifth appears labelled **Unexplained**. It is
meant to be embarrassing. Quietly forcing a decomposition to reconcile is the
one failure mode of a bridge that nobody can see.

### Line by line, what actually moved

The same question per line, where volume can be compared with itself. Everything
but volume is measured **per cover, per order or per day** — "we spent more on
food" is not a finding when more people were served, and per head it either is
or it is not.

### What breaks first

Four shocks of 10%, each on its own. A fall in takings takes the food bill down
with it and leaves the wages where they are, which is why a quiet month hurts
more than the takings line suggests. Where there is no profit, the headroom
table is replaced by the plainer sentence, because "takings can fall −14% before
the profit goes" is not a softer warning than "you are losing money" — it is a
false statement.

### What each line earns

Margin is compared against the weighted margin of **the lines that take money**.
Not the average of the lines, which lets a small line with a freak margin set a
standard the real business then fails. And not the group's own margin, which
housekeeping and maintenance drag below zero by design — judged against that,
every earning line looks sixty points above standard, which says nothing about
the line and everything about who pays for the cleaners.

### Did the profit turn into money?

What the tills charged against what they collected, and what suppliers invoiced
against what has been paid. Those two live in different systems and this is the
only screen they appear on together. A period can be profitable and still empty
the account.

Stock is missing from the cash cycle on purpose: no connected system values what
is on the shelf, so an inventory figure would be a guess in the same font as two
measured ones.

## What it cannot tell you

**Rooms.** In none of the five systems. Every figure here is the group without
its rooms business, and the break-even is the break-even of what is measured.

**Breakfast will always look ruinous.** The food is bought for every guest in
the house and only outside guests pay a fee that any system records. It is a
cost of the rooms — and the rooms are the line nothing reports.

**The previous period is the previous period**, not the same period last year. A
seasonal business will read a season change as a decline.
